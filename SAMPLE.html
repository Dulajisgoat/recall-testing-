How it works

You don't sign up.
You're matched — by a person, in a day.

Tell us the subject, the level, the exam board, when your child sits the paper, and what's gone wrong. We'll send a written recommendation within 24 hours — usually three tutors we'd suggest, with reasons. You pick. The first session is half-price and zero-commitment.

i.

You tell us the shape of the problem.

A ten-minute form, or a phone call if you'd rather. We need: subject, level, exam board, target grade, when the paper is, and what's been tried already. The more specific, the better the match.

Avg. time: 8 minutes · no account needed
ii.

We write back with three names.

Not an algorithmic shortlist — a recommendation written by Esme, who runs matching, with a sentence on why each tutor fits. You see their rate, their availability, and a sample of their teaching notes before you commit.

Response within 24 hours · usually 6
iii.

The first session is half-price.

60 minutes, on our annotation workspace. If the tutor isn't right — wrong tone, wrong pace, wrong vibe — say so. We'll re-match, and the second trial is on us. Roughly one in seven trials ends in a re-match; we expect that.

No card stored · no subscription · no minimum
iv.

You settle into a weekly slot.

Most students land on a regular weekly session — same tutor, same time, with a rolling Google-Doc-style lesson log you can read any time. Tutors upload worked solutions, set prep, and flag concerns to you directly. No portal, no dashboard.

Pause anytime · 24-hour cancellation

Every session runs on our annotation workspace — a shared whiteboard where tutor and student write, draw, and mark up exam questions together. Below: a real worked solution from a Year 13 Further Maths session last Tuesday. Tap between subjects to see how the same workspace adapts.

Edexcel · FP1 · January 2024 · Q7b 9 marks
Q Find the roots of z⁴ + 4 = 0 in the form a + bi, and show them on an Argand diagram.
01
z⁴ = −4 → z = (−4)¼
First, rewrite — we're looking for fourth roots of −4. Easier in polar form.
02
−4 = 4(cos π + i sin π) → |z| = 4¼ = √2
Modulus is √2. Argument of −4 is π (it sits on the negative real axis).
03
arg(zk) = (π + 2πk)/4, k = 0,1,2,3
Four roots, evenly spaced — divide the full 2π into four. This is the bit students forget.
04
z = √2 (cos π/4 + i sin π/4) = 1 + i
k=0 → π/4. So our first root is 1 + i. The other three follow by symmetry.
05
z = 1+i, −1+i, −1−i, 1−i
A square, centred on the origin, vertices at 45° intervals. Plot them — the diagram is worth two marks on its own.

Don't reach for the formula sheet first — write down what "fourth root" actually means geometrically. The square on the Argand diagram falls out for free.

AQA · Chemistry Paper 1 · June 2023 · Q5c 6 marks
Q 4.8 g of magnesium carbonate is added to 50 cm³ of 1.0 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. Calculate the volume of CO₂ produced at RTP. Give your answer to 2 significant figures.
01
MgCO₃ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
Always start with the balanced equation. 1 mol MgCO₃ gives 1 mol CO₂.
02
n(MgCO₃) = 4.8 / 84.3 = 0.0569 mol
Mr of MgCO₃: 24.3 + 12 + 48 = 84.3. Don't forget magnesium is 24.3, not 24.
03
n(HCl) = 1.0 × 0.050 = 0.050 mol → limiting
Two HCl per MgCO₃, so we need 0.114 mol HCl to react with all the carbonate. We only have 0.050 — acid runs out first.
04
n(CO₂) = 0.050 / 2 = 0.025 mol
Limiting reagent is HCl. 2 mol HCl → 1 mol CO₂, so halve it.
05
V = 0.025 × 24 = 0.60 dm³ (2 s.f.)
Molar gas volume at RTP is 24 dm³ mol⁻¹. The question wants 2 s.f. — don't lose the final mark by writing 0.6.

The trap is step 3. Half the cohort assumes magnesium carbonate is the limiting reagent because it's the "main" chemical. Always check both — the answer changes completely.

ISEB 11+ Pre-Test · Verbal Reasoning · Section 4 1 mark
Q If the code for WIRE is XJSF, what is the code for PORT?
01
W → X (shift +1)
Look at the first letter of each. W becomes X — that's a forward shift of one.
02
I → J, R → S, E → F (all +1)
Check the rest. Every letter shifts forward by exactly one. The rule is consistent — so apply it to PORT.
03
P → Q, O → P, R → S, T → U
Apply the same +1 rule to each letter of PORT, in order.
04
Answer: QPSU
Read it back to make sure. Q-P-S-U. The temptation in timed conditions is to skip the read-back — don't.

For 11+ code questions: find the rule with the first letter pair, then verify it on every other letter before you trust it. If two letters don't follow the same rule, you've picked the wrong one — start over.
